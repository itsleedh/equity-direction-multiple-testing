# Results

## Headline conclusion

No candidate in the verified flagship audit significantly beat the
target-majority baseline after Benjamini–Hochberg FDR correction. This is a
negative result: the tested evidence does not support presenting the project as
a profitable strategy or alpha-generating system.

## Public result audit

`reports/public/verified_flagship_runs.csv` is generated by
`scripts/build_public_summary.py` from five local archived
`performance_summary.csv` files. The script:

1. retains test rows only;
2. reads the stored majority-null p-values;
3. applies the repository's BH implementation across each run;
4. selects the weighted win rate for triple-barrier rows when applicable; and
5. counts a pass only when the corrected majority-null rejects and the tested
   win rate is above the majority rate.

The generated file also records a SHA-256 checksum for each local source
summary. The multi-gigabyte predictions and vendor data are deliberately not
part of the public release.

| Run | Test rows | OOS predictions | FDR passes vs 0.5 | Majority-null FDR rejections | Beats majority after FDR |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| PIT top-10 daily | 171 | 159,284 | 20 | 14 | **0** |
| FRED macro daily | 171 | 160,195 | 22 | 15 | **0** |
| CBOE SKEW daily | 171 | 159,901 | 22 | 16 | **0** |
| Static PIT-union daily | 171 | 308,019 | 42 | 47 | **0** |
| Long-history intraday 5m | 112 | 6,450,316 | 26 | 63 | **0** |

The majority-null test is two-sided. Its rejections in these runs are on the
underperformance side, which is why the rejection count can be positive while
the “beats majority” count remains zero.

## Experiment count

The legacy v1–v4 research summaries repeatedly report **0 FDR-significant
strategies out of 442 evaluated combinations**. That count is preserved in
local pre-release research records, but the codebase has no single experiment
manifest or generation script that reconstructs exactly the 442-row
denominator.

The public release therefore treats 442 as a **historical reported count, not
an independently reconstructed verified count**. It is not used as the
denominator of the final drift-aware audit above.

## Out-of-sample prediction count

The long-history intraday count is directly reproducible:

```text
sum(predictions)
over sample_type == "test"
in reports/output_intraday_long/performance_summary.csv
= 6,450,316
```

These are aggregated predictions across 112 strategy–symbol summary rows.
They are not 6,450,316 statistically independent observations, and they should
not be added to daily-run counts to manufacture a larger headline sample.

## Nominal significance versus corrected significance

The archived summaries include both raw binomial p-values and BH-adjusted
values against a 0.5 null. Several later runs have nonzero
`significant_after_fdr` counts against 0.5. Most of those results reflect
long/majority drift, not a strategy edge.

The final interpretation uses the majority baseline. Across the five flagship
runs, the stricter `beats_majority_after_fdr` count is zero in every run.

## Deflated Sharpe Ratio

`metrics/multiple_testing.py` and `reports/performance.py` calculate Deflated
Sharpe Ratio values using strategy-trial counts, observation count, skew, and
kurtosis. The implementation stores DSR as a diagnostic, but the repository
does not encode a universal DSR acceptance threshold. Reporting a DSR “pass
count” would therefore require a new methodological decision and is
intentionally omitted.

The historical conclusion does not rely on selecting the largest Sharpe or DSR
value.

## Drift and majority-class comparison

The 0.5 null asks whether direction accuracy differs from a fair coin. In a
rising sample, an always-up or long-biased rule can beat 50% without containing
security-specific predictive information.

The final gate instead compares with each row's observed target-majority rate.
The public audit shows 14 to 63 majority-null rejections per flagship run, but
zero rejections on the outperforming side. This supports the interpretation
that apparently significant direction results were explained by drift or
underperformance rather than robust predictive edge.

## Robustness findings

The historical records describe the following falsification outcomes:

- a fixed confidence threshold weakened under nested training-only selection;
- triple-barrier returns shrank after overlapping positions were simulated
  sequentially and inference used effective sample size;
- present-day top-ten membership backfilled into history inflated long-drift
  performance and created apparent significant rows;
- FRED macro and CBOE SKEW clusters were unstable under single-feature
  ablations or 10 bps cost stress;
- FINRA short-interest on/off runs both produced zero FDR-significant rows
  against the historical 0.5 gate in their common window; and
- long-history intraday results failed at 1 bp despite surviving at zero cost.

These claims come from the local research reports and their named configuration
and output directories. Only the flagship table and cost-survival table have
been rebuilt into small public machine-readable summaries.

## Interpretation

The negative result does not prove market efficiency or universal absence of
alpha. It means the evaluated candidates do not support an alpha claim after
the controls represented here. The useful output is the research system:
leakage canaries, temporal splitting, point-in-time masking, cost accounting,
multiple-testing correction, and explicit negative-result reporting.

## Source limitations

- Historical output directories are ignored because they total several
  gigabytes and include derived predictions tied to non-redistributed data.
- The raw input data are not redistributed.
- Five flagship runs are related robustness experiments, not independent
  replications.
- The 442 denominator lacks a reconstructable manifest.
- DSR has no encoded pass threshold.
- The source data and vendor revisions may change if fetched again.
